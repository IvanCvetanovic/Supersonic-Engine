# Porting HUSK's simulation

> **Record, started 10 September 2026, written as the work runs.** The owner's
> go-ahead, in their words: "first do husk. Do the engine gaps when needed,
> cross platform. After that you may proceed with wolf brigade and finall magic
> portals remake". The port targets HUSK (`Desktop/Test-Game`, crate
> `project-husk`) **pinned at `721e19e`**. The game keeps moving, and a port of a
> moving target silently goes stale, as the Wolf Brigade port did (see
> [2026-09-10-migration-readiness.md](2026-09-10-migration-readiness.md)).
> Re-pinning is a decision, and it gets an entry in the log below.

## What is being ported, and what it is held to

`src/sim/` is 19 files and about 7,300 lines of Rust. It is a 20 Hz fixed-tick
schedule of 22 systems chained in one order (`src/sim/mod.rs:110-143`), with its
own PCG32, nav grid, Dijkstra flow fields, FNV-1a state hash and snapshot. It
reads 58 RON files under `assets/`. It borrows nothing from Bevy but the ECS
containers, and it will borrow nothing from Supersonic's renderer either. It
becomes `games/husk/sim`, a `HuskSim` static library linking `SupersonicCore`,
in the same shape as `WolfBrigadeSim`.

**The standard is bit-for-bit**: the C++ port must produce the Rust sim's
`SimHash` on every tick of every scenario. HUSK's determinism policy
(`TECH_NOTES.md`, "Determinism policy") is what makes that reachable across
compilers and C runtimes, and it is stricter than anything the engine asks of
itself:

- f32 arithmetic uses only `+ - * /`, `sqrt`, min/max/clamp and the
  exactly-rounded `floor/ceil/round/abs`. No `sin/cos/pow`: libm varies by
  platform.
- Systems are single-threaded and chained, and iteration is in `SimId` order
  wherever order can affect a result. There is no hash-map iteration.
- The RNG is PCG32, owned in the repo.

That policy is why HUSK's sim, unlike the engine's physics, should cross from
Windows to Linux. The 10 September readiness record found that the engine's
`test_determinism` constant does not.

## Method

**An oracle outside both repositories' sources.** `games/husk/oracle/` is a
small Rust crate. It depends on `project-husk` by path (`../../../../Test-Game`,
with the `headless` feature) and dumps what the C++ side is checked against:
per-tick hashes for scripted scenarios, the parsed catalogs as raw f32 bits, and
the bits of the glam primitives on fixed inputs. HUSK's own repository is not
edited. Anything the oracle needs that is not public is re-derived from public
components.

**A divergence dump from day one, on both sides.** Each tick records the full
hash and sub-hashes per domain (units, buildings, sources, economy, mission),
plus a per-entity dump every Nth tick. A final hash after 10,000 ticks says
"wrong" and nothing else. The first differing tick and domain says which system
to read.

**Order of work, with one gate:**

1. rng + hash
2. data + the RON parser, with the **parity checkpoint**: catalogs compared as
   f32 bits and resolved ids, plus the glam primitives compared as bits
3. unit + map + flow
4. orders + movement. **The gate: `tests/determinism.rs`'s scripted M0 march,
   matched tick for tick over 10,000 ticks.** If it holds, what remains is grind.
   If it does not, the fault is in parsing or FP, with four files ported rather
   than nineteen.
5. combat
6. economy, building, difficulty and loot
7. hero
8. mission and scenario
9. snapshot

After step 9, the 96 Rust tests are ported as `test_husk_*` suites.

## Traps, written down before they bite

- **FP contraction.** `Vec2::dot` is `(x*x)+(y*y)`, and a fused `fma(x,x,y*y)`
  has different bits. `-ffp-contract=off` on GCC/clang, and on MSVC an explicit
  no-contraction setting whose effect a primitive test proves. No
  `-march=native`.
- **Transcribe glam 0.30.10, do not reimplement it.** `normalize()` is
  `self * length_recip()`, a multiply by `1/len` rather than a division by
  `len`, and those give different results.
- **Float to int.** Rust's `as` saturates and sends NaN to 0. C++'s
  `static_cast` is undefined out of range. Every float-to-int cast goes through
  one saturating helper.
- **`f32::max`/`min` ignore NaN**, like `std::fmax`/`fmin` and unlike
  `std::max`. `f32::clamp` passes NaN through.
- **Integers.** `wrapping_*` and `saturating_sub` are copied literally, and the
  shift in `rotate_right` is masked.
- **Order.** `BTreeMap` iterates in sorted-key order, so the port uses
  `std::map` wherever iteration order feeds a result. Entities live in a
  SimId-ordered store and are iterated in SimId order everywhere, which is
  stricter than Bevy's query order on purpose. A Rust system that depends on raw
  query order then shows up as a hash mismatch to read, rather than as a second
  source of nondeterminism.
- **Data.** Catalog ids come from the sorted *file paths*, not from the names
  inside. Name keys are `to_lowercase()`, which is per-byte only because every
  name is ASCII; the loader asserts that. Numbers are parsed with
  `std::from_chars`, never `strtof` or streams, which depend on the locale.

## Constraints recorded rather than solved

- **The data is copied**, 58 RON files into `games/husk/data`, as Wolf Brigade's
  JSON was. The copy is of `721e19e`.
- **`test_determinism` stays red on Linux during this work.** The owner decided
  on 10 September that the engine's determinism claim is to be cross-platform.
  That is engine work, and it is scheduled after the HUSK sim reaches parity.
  When it starts, the first step is to measure which libm call diverges first,
  before replacing anything.
- **The view comes after the sim.** Following the 19 August two-games record,
  the first view is deliberately ugly: boxes, no fog, no glow. Engine gaps are
  built when this port asks for them (the owner's rule), and each one gets a
  line here.
- **Engine gap, built: a box that is the colour of its material.** The `Cube`
  primitive is the engine's debug primitive, rainbow on purpose, and the shader
  multiplies albedo by vertex colour. Every box in HUSK's first screenshot was
  therefore a pink-to-green gradient.

  Changing `Cube` would restyle every scene that uses it, so `Box` is a new
  primitive instead: the cube's geometry built from `GenerateCube`, with white
  vertices. It resolves in `MeshRegistry` and gets the cube's hull in
  `ConvexHullCache`, so a collider cannot disagree with it. The inspector
  lists it, and `test_meshgen` pins it. This mirrors what `Quad` already did
  for 2D.
- **Engine gap: a camera cannot move on the tick and still move smoothly.**
  The renderer draws from `CameraComponent.position`/`front` rather than from
  the camera entity's transform. `InterpolationSystem` therefore cannot smooth
  a camera that a game moves on the tick. HUSK's camera moves per frame
  instead, and a click read on the tick is resolved against the camera as it
  stands. A replay reproduces an order only if the viewer's camera stands where
  the player's stood. Not yet built.
- **Engine gap: the mouse wheel is not part of a recorded tick.**
  `Input::Scroll()` is per frame and absent from `TickInput`, so wheel zoom
  could not be replayed even with the camera on the tick. It is the other half
  of the gap above and will be built with it. Not yet built.

## Log

- **10 Sep.** Pinned `721e19e`. The Rust suite was run as a baseline in WSL
  (Ubuntu 24.04, rustc 1.96.0): all 96 tests pass. glam is 0.30.10, ron 0.12.1.
- **10 Sep. The whole sim is ported, and the gate held on the first build.** All
  nineteen files went in at once rather than in the four-file stages planned.
  `drain_orders` alone reaches buildings, heroes, economy and combat, so a
  partial port had nothing to run. The per-domain sub-hashes were there to
  localise a failure, and none was needed:
  - **`test_husk_foundation`, 69 checks.** RON, Rust's cast and NaN rules,
    4,000 glam/PCG32 results, and 296 lines of catalog and mission values, all
    bit-identical to the oracle.
  - **`test_husk_scenarios`, 15 checks.** Every tick of five scripted scenarios
    matches the Rust sim's own `SimHash`: the M0 march (10,000 ticks, the gate),
    the 20v20 battle (2,400), the macro sandbox (4,000, hero death and revival
    included), Heartwood (5,000) and First Light (4,000), both missions
    installed from their RON.
  - **Both toolchains, identical results.** MSVC 14.50 on the UCRT and GCC 13.3
    on glibc 2.39. HUSK's determinism policy does what the engine's physics
    does not: the same bits on both C runtimes.

  **A green first run is when the harness most needs doubting,** so it was made
  to fail twice. Both were one-off g++ builds of a copy, with the real trees
  untouched:
  - **Contraction allowed:** `-march=haswell -ffp-contract=fast`, with the same
    `-march` and `-ffp-contract=off` as the control, which stays green. The
    primitive check fails on its first input, where `clamp_length_max` is one
    ulp off. The scenarios diverge at ticks 39 (M0), 6 (battle) and 17 (macro).
    The flag is load-bearing on any FMA-capable target, not hygiene.
  - **One constant nudged:** the separation gain moved from 5.0 to 5.0001. The
    scenarios diverge at ticks 36, 3, 25 and 3714 (Heartwood).

  Two Bevy semantics are carried over on purpose. Deferred `Commands` apply at
  the end of the system that issued them. Removal from the SimIndex is separate
  from despawning. Rust's left-to-right argument evaluation is written out
  wherever two RNG draws feed one `Vec2`.
- **10 Sep. HUSK's own tests became 63 more scenarios.** The `.scn` language
  grew until it could state the setups of the Rust suite:
  - **World edits at any tick:** units, buildings (as sites or built), sources,
    items, the hero, essence, anima, affinity, research, hp, hero level,
    inventory and production queues.
  - **Content:** missions from RON beside the scenario and hand-drawn maps
    (plateaus, ramps, obstacles, polygons).
  - **Hooks and saves:** a test-only mission hook, and `snapshot`, which saves
    and then restores into a fresh world built with another seed.

  Both sides read the same lines: the oracle through the game's own
  functions, the port through `Scenario.cpp`.

  `games/husk/golden/make_scenarios.py` writes the scenarios. They cover the
  leash, taunt, the chain arc, the balance doctrine, loot, research, the
  tower, the hero kit, missions and their triggers, difficulty, terrain and
  saves. **All 68 scenarios, 76,521 ticks, agree with the Rust sim bit for bit,
  on MSVC/UCRT and GCC/glibc alike** (`test_husk_scenarios`, 142 checks).

  The Rust suite passes all 96 of its tests at `721e19e`, and the port
  reproduces that sim's hash on every tick of these setups. The Rust
  assertions therefore hold for the port on these setups too, without being
  rephrased. They were not transcribed: an assertion checks one fact, while the
  hash checks all of them.

  The first run caught one scenario-language bug that was not in the sim:
  `kind=#65535` asked for a raw id, but `#` starts a comment, so both sides
  read an empty name. Raw ids are now `@N`. A scenario that throws is now one
  failure rather than the end of the suite.

  **Agreement cannot show that a line does anything.** A verb that was a no-op
  on both sides would agree just as well. So `golden/sensitivity.py` drops each
  of the 540 script lines in turn and reruns the oracle.

  493 lines change the per-tick hash sequence. Comparing only the final hash
  was not enough: a fight that ends with everyone regenerated converges, and it
  hid eight real effects.

  The other 47 are inert, and each is inert on purpose:
  - **Refusals the scenario is there to check:** malformed orders, research
    while poor or while another is running, the research cap, a unit whose
    affinity is not yet met, a revive the player cannot pay for, cancels on an
    empty queue or a bad id, a poultice at full health, and using a piece of
    equipment.
  - **The queue cap:** of twelve `produce` lines, dropping any one still fills
    the seven slots.
  - **Four `snapshot`s:** a save and restore that moved the hash would be the
    bug.
  - **The default difficulty,** stated explicitly.

  One line is merely redundant: in `m5_hero_does_everything`, `place` already
  sends the builder, so the explicit `build` after it adds nothing.
