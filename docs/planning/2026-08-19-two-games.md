# Could Supersonic host these two games?

**Wolf Brigade: no. HUSK: partly.** The two answers fail for completely different
reasons, and neither reason is one of the limitations the engine is best known for. The
8-light cap, the box-box-as-AABB narrowphase, the missing CCD and the missing navmesh do
not block either game. What blocks them is that Supersonic is an *application* with an
editor fused into it, and neither of these is a scene — they are programs.

---

## 1. Wolf Brigade

### Could it be ported today? No.

Not "no, that would take a while" — no in the sense that the thing you finished would
not be the game. Wolf Brigade ships on Android and its primary input is touch
(`export_presets.cfg`; `project.godot [input_devices]` sets
`emulate_mouse_from_touch=false` deliberately). Supersonic creates its window and
surface through GLFW (`src/platform/Window.cpp:32-58`), which has no Android backend;
`src/platform/AndroidNativeApp.cpp` is 53 lines of `ANativeWindow*` holder and is
filtered out of every non-Android build (`CMakeLists.txt:165-167`). There is no touch
concept anywhere — `RawInputState` (`src/core/Input.hpp:74-83`) is
`keys[349]`, `mouseButtons[8]`, one `mousePosition`, `scroll`, one pad — and the pointer
that drives the in-game UI is ImGui's mouse (`src/editor/EditorLayer.cpp:289-293`). The
game's core selection verbs, hold-then-drag to marquee versus quick-drag to pan
(`scripts/input/input_controller.gd:110-141`), have no expressible form, and its autosave
hangs off Android lifecycle notifications (`scripts/main.gd:87-102`) that have no
counterpart in the engine at all.

Port it to desktop-with-a-mouse and you have shipped a different product. Everything
below is a distant second.

### The blockers, ranked

**1. Android, and touch — MISSING ENTIRELY.** Not a gap in a subsystem; there is no
subsystem. A port needs a native activity entry point, surface creation and loss/recreate,
a lifecycle model, a touch type threaded through `RawInputState` → `Input` → the UI
pointer, and tap/drag/hold arbitration re-derived from scratch. Two things make it worse
than it sounds: packaging is a *copy of the running executable*
(`src/editor/GamePackager.cpp:50`), so the shipped binary contains ImGui, the inspector,
the content browser and the packager itself; and textures upload as uncompressed RGBA8
with no BC/ASTC/KTX path (`src/renderer/TextureRegistry.cpp:110-190`), which is exactly
the memory profile a mobile target cannot afford. Months, with no partial credit.

**2. No seam for game code — MISMATCH OF KIND.** `SupersonicApp` exposes a constructor
and `Run()`; `private:` begins at `src/core/SupersonicApp.hpp:37` and
`entt::registry m_registry` sits at `:67`. The frame body
(`src/core/SupersonicApp.cpp:495-635`) is a fixed sequence with no callback and no
system-registration point. Wolf Brigade's nine singletons — `Lane`, `Selection`,
`Commands`, `WaveDirector`, `Projectiles`, `FloatingNumbers`, `Snapshot`, `Audio`,
`SaveData` — have nowhere to live except inside `SupersonicApp.cpp`. The one game-code
hook in the engine hands each scripted entity a POD block of its own transform plus four
function-pointer groups (`src/core/ScriptPluginApi.h:107-122`), with no spawn, no destroy,
no component access, no per-entity authored state, and no way to name another entity. So
the game is not *ported to* the engine, it is *compiled into* it — and hot-reloadable
scripting, the engine's headline gameplay feature, stops being part of the story. Cost in
lines is small: expose the registry, or add a layer interface with per-frame callbacks. Cost
in identity is not — it changes what the engine *is*, which is why this is a mismatch of kind
and not a missing feature. That is the expensive category throughout this report: a missing
feature is bought with weeks, a mismatch of kind is bought by conceding an assumption the rest
of the engine was built on.

**3. Orthographic 2D, and flat unlit colour — MISMATCH OF KIND (price them together).**
`CameraComponent::getProjectionMatrix()` hardcodes `glm::perspective`
(`src/core/Components.hpp:172-176`); the only `glm::ortho` in the entire tree is the
shadow projection (`src/renderer/ShadowCascades.cpp:198` — I grepped). There is one scene
pipeline, full Cook-Torrance PBR, whose hemisphere ambient deliberately tints the top and
bottom of a surface differently (`assets/shaders/shader.frag:383-396`) — on a wall of flat
quads that is precisely the content that makes it obvious. `MaterialComponent`
(`src/core/Components.hpp:232-251`) has no unlit flag and no shading-model field, and the
engine creates three graphics pipelines total with no variant system. `#4a90d9` will not
come out as `#4a90d9`. The ortho branch is genuinely small; the unlit path is a second
pipeline. Until both land, every "2D rectangle" is a 3D plane under perspective, and the
per-layer z offsets the game needs (ring / body / bar / label) each sit at a different
apparent scale.

**4. Alpha on world content — PRESENT BUT INADEQUATE.** Blending is implemented
(`src/renderer/VulkanPipeline.cpp:129-138`) and used exactly once, for the editor grid
(`src/renderer/VulkanRenderer.cpp:301` — the only `blendEnable = true` outside BloomPass
in the repo). The scene pipeline leaves it false, and `shader.frag:407` computes an alpha
the blend state throws away. This is not polish: a constructing building at alpha 0.45 and
a placement ghost tinted green or red are the *only* cues for two game states
(`scripts/entities/building.gd:245`, `scripts/systems/build_placement.gd:47`). Cost: a blend
state, a transparent bucket, a depth sort and a per-material opaque/transparent split — days,
not weeks, because the pipeline machinery already exists and the grid proves it works against
the same render pass.

**5. Text in the world — MISSING.** Every unit and building carries a name label, and
damage numbers float over whatever was hit. `UITextComponent` is screen-anchored by
construction — the comment at `src/core/Components.hpp:465-467` says so outright — and the
ABI's UI block offers `setText`/`setVisible`/`setFill` with no position setter. Compounding
it: one 16px Inter atlas (`src/editor/EditorFonts.cpp:51-63`) rescaled for everything, in a
game running from 22px rows to an 88px PAUSED. Cost: a projected-text pass is ~50 lines, but
it has to live inside `EditorLayer::buildGameView` because that is the only place a game can
draw inside the ImGui frame — which folds it back into blocker 2.

**6. Per-entity colour and visibility from game code — PRESENT BUT INADEQUATE.**
`MaterialComponent::albedoColor` and `RenderableComponent::isVisible` are plain writable
fields the renderer reads (`src/core/Components.hpp:233`, `:270`). Nothing game-facing can
reach them. Hit flash, selection ring, HP-bar colour lerp and ghost validity are all state
reads with no other channel; the closest hack is scaling to zero.

**7. UI layout and runtime rebuild — MISSING (layout) / INADEQUATE (the rest).** The entire
layout model is nine anchors plus a pixel offset against a 1080 reference
(`src/core/UICanvas.hpp:15-25`, `39-62`). No containers, no rows, no scroll — so the
runtime-built Armory list has to fit on one screen, and every variable-length row rebuilt
from `meta.json` gets its offsets recomputed by hand. Worse, z-order is fixed by system:
all panels, then all buttons, then all text (`src/core/UISystem.cpp:46/75/112`), so a modal
pause overlay has every menu button in the game showing through it. And `UIInput::Update`'s
"did the UI take this click" result is called as a bare statement and discarded
(`src/core/UISystem.cpp:42`), so the world acts on clicks the HUD already consumed.

**8. Persistence — MISSING, and the serializer cannot be extended to cover it.** The game
persists settings, meta-progression and a full mid-match entity graph with a cross-type
`sid` relink pass (~350 lines across `scripts/systems/save.gd` and `snapshot.gd`).
Supersonic has no save concept and no user-data path — writes are relative to the working
directory (`src/core/SupersonicApp.cpp:86`) and the manifest anchor and the CWD anchor
already disagree (ARCHITECTURE.md §9). Critically the ingredients cannot be reused:
`ComponentCodec` is a closed if-chain of exactly 18 component keys (I counted the
`node.Has("...")` branches, `src/core/ComponentCodec.cpp:307-493`) with no registration
hook, so a game-defined `hp` / `carry` / `train_queue` is silently dropped by the scene
writer, the prefab writer and the Play snapshot alike.

**9. The camera actively fights the game — MISMATCH OF KIND.** Worse than "no way to pan
it". In play mode `CameraSystem::Update` runs every frame
(`src/core/SupersonicApp.cpp:537`) and `EditorLayer` sets hovered and focused to `true`
unconditionally in game mode (`src/editor/EditorLayer.cpp:269-270`, under the comment "A game
owns the mouse and keyboard outright"). `CameraSystem` then polls raw GLFW WASD/Space/Shift to
fly the primary camera and makes right-button-drag a free-look
(`src/core/CameraSystem.cpp:29-36`, `:42-57`) — colliding head-on with
right-click-to-command. There is no off switch, and `CameraComponent` owns its own position
and is never synced from a transform.

**10. Audio one-shots — PRESENT BUT WRONG for this use.** Sound is a property of an entity,
not an event. `AudioSystem.cpp:75-84` explicitly restarts a finished non-looping voice, so a
one-shot repeats forever unless something clears it — and nothing script-reachable can,
since there is no audio block in the ABI. No bus, no master gain, no mute; every source is
3D-attenuated against the camera, so a UI click has to be parented to the listener to sound
flat.

### What Wolf Brigade would exercise that the engine has never been asked to do

- **Shipping somewhere that is not a Windows desktop.** Every assumption from GLFW down,
  plus a texture format story, plus a binary that is not the editor.
- **Being a product rather than a scene viewer.** Settings that persist, meta-progression, a
  resumable mid-match save, four top-level screens, a back button, autosave on backgrounding.
  Nothing in the engine has ever written a byte of player data.
- **Being mostly UI.** Three of four top-level screens are pure Control trees, and the command
  bar destroys and rebuilds its whole button set on every selection or resource change
  (`scripts/ui/bottom_bar.gd:59-84`). Against that: three widgets, absolute anchors, no
  z-order, one font, no word wrap (`CalcTextSizeA` is called with `wrap_width 0.0f`), one
  pointer, and a discarded input-capture result.
- **Being data-driven by a designer, not a programmer.** Ten JSON files re-parsed per match
  and cross-validated. The engine has no authored-parameter concept at all — `ScriptComponent`
  carries a name string and three engine-owned floats (`src/core/Components.hpp:416-429`).
- **Hundreds of identical tiny quads and fifty-plus text runs per frame.** The exact content
  shape instancing exists for; `drawIndexed(..., 1, ...)` with a literal 1
  (`src/core/RenderSystem.cpp:228`) and no material sort.

## 2. HUSK

### Could it be ported today? Partly — the simulation yes, the game no.

HUSK splits unusually cleanly, and the half that sounds terrifying is the half that costs
nothing. `D:/Test Game/src/sim` (~7,600 lines) is pure and self-contained: its own PCG32,
its own 100×100 nav grid, its own Dijkstra flow fields, its own spatial hash, its own FNV-1a
hash oracle, its own snapshot. It borrows nothing from Bevy but the ECS containers and would
borrow nothing from Supersonic either. It ports as plain C++, and that is also why the
engine's most-quoted weaknesses never touch it: no navmesh and no pathfinding do not matter
to a game that brings its own (`src/sim/flow.rs`), and sphere/box-only collision with an
unconditional y=0 ground plane does not matter to a game whose `TECH_NOTES.md:84` says a
physics engine "would fight determinism." Even the determinism requirement is nearly free:
25 GPU-free test executables already link `SupersonicCore`, and `tests/CMakeLists.txt:8-11`
states the property in its own words.

What HUSK cannot do today is *run*. There is nowhere to put a 20 Hz loop.

### The blockers, ranked

**1. No hosting seam — MISSING, and the only true blocker.** The sim is one `.chain()`ed
22-system schedule advancing exactly one tick per `FixedUpdate` at a compile-time `SIM_HZ`
(`src/sim/mod.rs:34-36`, `:110-143`, `:174-181`). Supersonic has one fixed step and it wraps
`PhysicsSystem::Update` alone (`src/core/SupersonicApp.cpp:547-562`); scripts, animation,
particles and audio all take the raw frame delta immediately afterwards (`:565-568`). With
`m_registry` private and `EditorLayer` a hard `unique_ptr` member (`SupersonicApp.hpp:50`),
the port is a *second executable* re-implementing the init and the frame body around
`VulkanRenderer::DrawFrame` — giving up the editor, PlayMode and hot reload to do it. One
trap worth naming: do not copy the engine's own fixed step. It clamps the frame delta to
100 ms *before* accumulating (`SupersonicApp.cpp:504`) and zeroes the remainder once five
steps are spent (`:561-562`), so under load it discards simulated time continuously. That
would not break bit-exactness — state at tick N stays a function of seed and tick-keyed
orders — but every mission timer, research countdown and production queue would run in slow
motion.

**2. No blended surface in the scene pass — MISSING, and engine-source-only.** Nearly
everything else here a game linking the library can write for itself. This it cannot:
`VulkanRenderer::DrawFrame` owns the offscreen render pass recording end to end
(`VulkanRenderer.cpp:962-1002`) with no hook to inject a draw. The consequence is not a
fidelity item — it is **fog of war**, a core RTS mechanic drawn as a terrain-conforming
`AlphaMode::Blend` overlay (`src/client/fog.rs`, `src/client/scene.rs:492`). The vignette,
the hero glow decal, the rain sheet and every flame quad go with it. Cost is modest in
absolute terms — a second pipeline instance, a transparent bucket, a back-to-front sort, a
per-material flag — and unavoidable: it is an engine-source change no linking game can route
around.

**3. GPU resource lifetime — MISSING, and the highest-leverage change on the list.**
`MeshRegistry` and `TextureRegistry` are write-once. I read both headers: neither exposes an
update, a free or an eviction, and their storage and lookup maps are private
(`src/renderer/MeshRegistry.hpp:29-58`, `src/renderer/TextureRegistry.hpp:25-60`);
`Acquire`/`Upload` return the cached id on a key hit. So HUSK's 240×240 fog texture,
rewritten in place every frame, has no path at all — same key is a no-op, fresh key leaks a
`VkImage` per frame — and every mission transition leaks its terrain mesh, fog mesh and
minimap image permanently. Note the interaction: fixing (2) without (3) still does not give
you fog of war. Cost: `Release` and `Replace` on both registries, plus tracking GPU lifetimes
across frames in flight so a freed image is not still referenced by a submitted command
buffer — the one genuinely fiddly item on this list, and the one with the most downstream
value.

**4. Presentation interpolation — MISSING.** HUSK carries `SimPos { prev, cur }` purely so the
client can lerp by `overstep_fraction()` every frame (`src/client/interp.rs`). Supersonic has
exactly one authoritative `TransformComponent` per entity, written by scripts, the gizmo,
`CameraSystem` and physics, and read straight by the renderer — no sim/render split, no
published overstep fraction. A 20 Hz RTS presented without interpolation stutters visibly.
Small in code, structural in consequence, and it needs the per-frame hook from (1). It is
also where the "nothing engine-side writes a sim position" rule actually gets satisfied.

**5. The glTF importer produces nothing usable for this model library — INADEQUATE.** None of
the 58 GLBs is skinned. `GltfLoader.cpp:685-686` builds clips only when the skin-derived joint
map is non-empty, so zero skins means zero clips; `:632` bakes each non-skinned node's world
matrix into its vertices; and `MeshRegistry.cpp:103-138` merges every primitive into one
`MeshData` under one cache key. Each unit therefore arrives as a single welded statue with one
material — and the material *names* are the game's team-colour and affinity contract
(`GAME_SPEC.md:340`). The workaround is real but large: tinygltf is on the PUBLIC include path
and `MeshRegistry::Upload` is public, so the port brings its own importer, one mesh per
driver-node group, a child entity each, and its own clip sampler. Roughly 300-500 lines plus a
clip format — and it multiplies the entity count by about six.

**6. Draw scale — INADEQUATE.** ~700-800 scenery props plus up to 200 units, times ~6
part-entities each after (5), against one `pushConstants` plus one `drawIndexed` per visible
entity with no instancing and no material sort (`src/core/RenderSystem.cpp:184-229`) — re-run
per shadow cascade in `RenderDepthOnly`. Frustum culling exists and helps in both passes.
Feasible on a modern CPU, tight at 60, and the fix does not fit a push-constant-per-draw
layout without changing the layout.

**7. No emissive term — MISSING, and the cheapest high-value change here.** `EMISSIVE` is a
named material on 29 of the 58 models and is the game's entire affinity and team-state visual
language, driven deliberately above 1.0 to trip bloom. I grepped `src/` and `assets/shaders/`
for "emissive": nothing. Everything downstream already exists — R16G16B16A16Sfloat target,
threshold-1.0 bright pass with a soft knee, Reinhard rolloff — and `PushConstantData` has 24
spare bytes of its guaranteed 128 (`src/renderer/VulkanPipeline.hpp:75-86`). One vec4 and one
add in `shader.frag`.

**8. No distance fog, no grading, no runtime control of the look — INADEQUATE.** The HDR post
stack is better than its reputation; what is genuinely absent is distance fog and colour
grading, with bloom threshold/knee/intensity/exposure fixed as compile-time constants
(`BloomPass.hpp:50-53`). Fog matters most: without it a 120×120 map reads at full contrast to
its far edge and loses the depth cueing that makes an RTS legible.

**9. Live-state serialization — MISSING, but game-owned.** HUSK's `SAVE_VERSION 6` snapshot
round-trips RNG stream position, the id allocator and the undrained order queue. The engine's
nearest equivalents are traps, not foundations: `PlayMode` snapshots the *authored scene* to
restore the editor on Stop, and `TimeTravelDebugger` is a frame-keyed transform/velocity
rewind. Neither is a player-facing save.

**10. The editor grid draws in shipped games.** `VulkanRenderer.cpp:978-983` binds the grid
pipeline and draws unconditionally at the end of the scene pass, and `EditorLayer::buildGameView`
cannot suppress it. Small, but it is a visible artifact across an RTS map.

### What HUSK would exercise that the engine has never been asked to do

- **A fixed-tick simulation as the authority, with rendering as a derived view.** The engine has
  one clock concept and one transform per entity. This is the deepest thing on either list, and
  it is what "gameplay authority" should have meant.
- **Reproducibility as a tested property.** Nothing in Supersonic has ever been deterministic,
  and its own code violates the rules HUSK lives by: script `elapsed` accumulates from variable
  frame deltas (`ScriptEngine.cpp:246`) and drives `std::sin` off that clock (`:256-257`). No
  hash oracle, no tick counter, no CI gate on simulation reproducibility — the validation gate
  checks render correctness.
- **Being a library consumer rather than an application.** HUSK is the first thing that wants
  `SupersonicCore` and not `SupersonicApp`. The static-library split half-anticipated this;
  nothing above it did.
- **GPU resources with a lifetime.** Build a 14,641-vertex terrain, a fog mesh and a minimap
  image per mission, then throw them away. The engine has never freed anything — the registries
  only grow for the process lifetime.
- **A HUD made of images.** 32 PNGs, nine-sliced panels, portraits, command glyphs, a minimap
  with a fog layer. `UISystem` draws `AddRectFilled` and `AddText` and nothing else.
- **Spawn and destroy at production rates.** `PrefabSerializer::InstantiatePrefab` is the only
  runtime spawn-from-asset path and it re-opens and re-parses the file on every call with no
  cache (`src/core/PrefabSerializer.cpp:49-89`).

## 3. Together

The two games agree on exactly one structural thing, and it is the thing the plan does not
name: **there is no seam.** Wolf Brigade needs somewhere to put nine singletons; HUSK needs
somewhere to put a 20 Hz schedule. Both answers today are "inside `SupersonicApp.cpp`", which
means both ports stop being ports and become forks.

Past that they stress disjoint halves and barely overlap:

| | Wolf Brigade | HUSK |
|---|---|---|
| Hardest problem | Platform + presentation model | Simulation authority + resource lifetime |
| Renderer demand | Ortho, unlit, blend, world-space text | Blend, emissive, fog, instancing, importer |
| Physics demand | None — no collider in the project | None — deliberately excluded |
| Lights used | Zero (unlit) | 2 directional + ambient, 0 point |
| Determinism | Not required | The whole architecture |
| Verdict | No | Partly |

The only render change both need and neither can write for itself is **blending**.

The most striking result is what is absent from both columns. Neither game approaches eight
lights. Neither has a rotated crate. Neither needs CCD, sleeping, a navmesh or a pathfinder.
Two RTSs — the genre that ought to stress physics and lighting hardest — ask for neither.

## 4. What this changes about the roadmap

Reading the workstreams as written: **A** authoring pipeline (drag-and-drop asset assignment,
`SceneManager` with runtime `loadScene`, AssetDatabase with ids and `Invalidate`/`Release`,
`AssetRoot()`, packaging the real scene); **B** gameplay authority (ABI widening — per-entity
state, authored parameters, `spawnPrefab`/`destroy`, velocity/forces, collision and trigger
callbacks — plus collider offsets and layer masks); **C** observability and CI, largely
shipped; **D** visuals (IBL + skybox, mipmaps/anisotropy, the transparent pipeline with opaque
material sorting and instanced particles, windowed falloff, bloom pyramid); **E** physics
(SAT/OBB, then CCD, then multi-iteration with warm starting and sleeping, then deleting the
invisible ground plane); **F** scaling (clustered forward with light culling, cull-once shadow
caster list, batched fenced uploads, per-frame render targets, opaque draw sorting).

### Validated

**One line in D is the best-supported item in the entire plan** — and it is not the one D is
sold on. The transparent-pipeline item, specced as a second pipeline from the same shaders with
an authored `blendMode` on `MaterialComponent`, opaque draws sorted by material and transparent
sorted back-to-front, is exactly what both games need and the only render change common to
both. But D is described as the renderer's *visual* step with the highest quality return per
week. The games say it is not a quality item at all: it is fog of war, a build-placement ghost
and a construction state — mechanics that do not exist without it. Unbundle it from IBL and the
skybox, which neither game asks for, and reclassify it.

**C, retroactively and hard.** The static-library split plus 25 GPU-free suites is precisely the
substrate HUSK's 10,000-tick determinism and balance suites need, and `tests/CMakeLists.txt:8-11`
already articulates the property. One correction worth acting on: the GPU-freeness is
*linker-incidental*, not enforced. HUSK gets it as a build error via `--no-default-features`;
here a stray renderer reference silently makes a test binary need a GPU. That is a
target-discipline convention, not a rewrite.

**A's runtime half — not its editor half.** `SceneManager` with a deferred `RequestLoad`, a
deferred `loadScene` plus a `PersistentComponent` tag (which is exactly Wolf Brigade's
cross-scene `Snapshot.pending` carrier), `AssetRoot()`, and `Invalidate`/`Release` on the asset
database are all validated by both games. What is not validated is what A leads with, and what
the "if you only do three things" list puts at number one: drag-and-drop asset assignment in the
inspector. Neither game would ever use it. Wolf Brigade authors nothing in an editor; HUSK builds
its worlds programmatically from `.map.ron`. The priority inversion is *inside* A.

### Over-valued

**E, almost entirely.** Wolf Brigade contains no collider, no `Area2D` and no physics body
anywhere in the project — combat, picking and movement are 1-D arithmetic on x. HUSK excludes a
physics engine on purpose and would be actively harmed by one: a sim entity given a collider to
make engine picking work would be pushed by the solver and desync. SAT/OBB, CCD, warm starting
and sleeping buy these two games precisely nothing. The one part either game wants is the *last*
item in a strictly ordered 3-4 week sequence — deleting the unconditional ground plane and
putting a `PhysicsSettings` in its place — which both games need purely as hazard removal. Pull
that item out and ship it alone; it is cheap and it is the only part of E these games validate.

**F's headline.** Clustered forward with real light culling is the least relevant item in F.
HUSK's shipped missions use two directional lights plus ambient and zero point lights; Wolf
Brigade is unlit. The cap is a real defect — `if (count >= kMaxLights) break;` in plain pool
order (`VulkanRenderer.cpp:516`) silently extinguishes an arbitrary light — but it is not what
stops either game. F's *body* is well aimed: the cull-once shadow caster list, batched fenced
uploads and opaque draw sorting are exactly HUSK's problems. F is right in content and wrong in
emphasis.

**B, and this is the sharpest correction in the report.** B is called "gameplay authority" and
is, item for item, *script-ABI* authority: per-entity state, authored parameters,
`spawnPrefab`/`destroy`, get/setVelocity and `addForce`, collision enter/stay/exit. Every one of
those serves entity-scale behaviour — a character that jumps, a crate that reacts. Neither of
these games is entity-scale. Wolf Brigade's logic is nine singletons owning collections; HUSK's
is 22 chained systems reading the whole world at a fixed rate. Both abandon the script layer
entirely and neither gets closer to running if you ship all of B. Worse, two of its items are
*negatives* for HUSK: `addForce` and velocity on entities the sim owns is exactly the coupling
that desyncs a deterministic tick. B is not wrong — it is mis-named. It is character scripting,
not gameplay authority, and calling it the latter is what let the actual authority problem stay
invisible.

The same is true of the "if you only do three things" list: number one (drag-and-drop assignment)
and number three (velocity, forces and collision callbacks on the ABI) are both things neither
game would use. That is not a failure of the audit — it was written for an author sitting in the
editor, and both of these games are programs that never open it. But it means the top-three is
answering a different question than "can this engine host a game."

### Missing from the plan entirely

1. **An application seam.** A place for game code to run in the frame without editing
   `SupersonicApp.cpp` — Wolf Brigade's second blocker and HUSK's only one. It is in no
   workstream. The plan knows the *adjacent* fact: that `VulkanRenderer` includes editor headers,
   that the scene pipeline points at the editor's offscreen render pass
   (`SupersonicApp.cpp:126-127`), and that a `SUPERSONIC_RUNTIME_ONLY` build is gated on a
   UiBackend split. But it frames all of it as *subtraction* — get the editor out of the shipped
   binary. Both games need *addition*: get a game into the loop. Same coupling, opposite
   direction, and only one direction is written down.
2. **A fixed-tick game clock, with a sim/render split and interpolation.** Nothing in A-F. The
   only fixed step is physics-internal and discards simulated time under load. This is the item
   HUSK is entirely built on and the one with no analogue anywhere in the engine.
3. **Instanced draws for scene entities.** D has instanced *particles*; D and F both have opaque
   draw *sorting*. Instancing keyed by mesh+material — the actual wall for hundreds of identical
   quads or ~2,000 prop and part entities — is nowhere, and it does not fit the
   push-constant-per-draw layout without changing it.
4. **Game-defined components in the serializer.** The codec is on the list as architectural debt,
   with an engine-correctness framing — a key-array guard against forgetting a field, and a
   declarative descriptor table later. Neither opens the format to a game's own component types,
   and both games need exactly that: Wolf Brigade's mid-match save and HUSK's `SAVE_VERSION 6`
   snapshot are made of components the codec will never name.

And one framing point: A-F is organised by engine subsystem. Both games say the binding constraint
is architectural shape — the engine is an application with an editor fused into it, and a game is
not currently a thing that can exist beside it. No amount of progress *within* the subsystems
changes that.

---

## 5. The honest recommendation

Neither of these is a good first game for this engine, and Wolf Brigade is the worse fit by a wide
margin: it needs a platform that does not exist, an input modality that does not exist, and a flat
unlit 2D presentation model that is effectively a second renderer — while the parts Supersonic
could host today, JSON loading and greyed-out buttons, are the parts that were never hard. HUSK is
both the better fit and the more instructive one, because its scariest half costs the engine
nothing — 7,600 lines of deterministic simulation that port as plain C++ and never touch a
Supersonic subsystem — and its demands land squarely on the four things missing from the plan: a
hosting seam, resource lifetime, a blended pass and draw scaling. But the target that would prove
most is smaller than either game: take HUSK's sim as it is, run it headless under CTest against
`SupersonicCore` (the pattern exists already, 25 suites deep), and put a deliberately ugly view on
top — flat boxes for units, no fog, no glow, no animation, no HUD. That single exercise forces the
seam, a fixed-tick host with interpolation, spawn and destroy at RTS rates, and a mission
transition that must free what it uploaded, without needing transparency, a custom importer or one
icon. If it holds 200 units, the engine has earned the right to attempt the presentation layer; if
it cannot, no amount of IBL, oriented-box narrowphase or light culling was ever going to matter.
